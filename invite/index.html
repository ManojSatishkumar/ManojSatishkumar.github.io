What I speak about

Six sessions, straight from the job

Pick one of these, combine two, or tell me what your students need and I will shape a session around it.

How to actually prepare for interviews

What companies test and why, how to build a study plan you can hold for months, how much data structures really matter next to projects, how a resume gets read in twenty seconds, and how referrals and applications work on the inside. I have sat on both sides of the table.

Industry insights from inside the room

What a real engineering team looks like day to day — sprints, code review, design docs, releases, on-call, and what happens when your code is serving millions of people. The parts of the job that decide your growth are rarely the parts you practise in college.

What is expected of you after graduation

The honest version of your first year: what a manager actually expects in the first ninety days, the distance between a college project and production code, why reading code beats writing it early on, and the habits that quietly separate the freshers who get trusted from the ones who do not.

Junior engineer to architect, step by step

My own path, without the polish — from a junior developer to Staff Engineer at Walmart and Software Architect at Walt Disney. Which decisions compounded, which years I wasted, when to go deep versus broad, and what changes about the work at every level.

How to track and steer your own career

Nobody is going to run your career for you. Keeping a record of your own work, choosing what to learn next when everything looks urgent, building visible proof through projects and writing, asking for feedback that is actually useful, and reviewing yourself every six months.

Jury panels and project reviews

As I did at Project Space, I am glad to judge hackathons and project expos, review student work team by team, and give the kind of specific feedback that sends them back to the keyboard with something to fix. Prize distribution and closing remarks included.
