Let's set expectations first

Most disappointment around referrals comes from a mismatch in what people think they are getting. So here it is plainly.

What a referral does

  • Gets your resume in front of a recruiter who will actually open it, instead of sitting in a pile of thousands.
  • Adds a layer of trust. The company knows an existing employee thought you were worth flagging.
  • Often moves faster than a cold career-site application, because referred profiles are tracked separately.
  • Gives you a real person to ask about the team, the tech stack and what the interview loop looks like.

What it does not do

  • It does not guarantee an interview call. The hiring team still screens your profile against the role.
  • It does not skip any interview round, and it does not influence the outcome of one. You will be evaluated exactly like everyone else.
  • It does not fix a resume that does not match the job description. A referral amplifies your profile, it does not rewrite it.
  • It does not come with a timeline I control. Once submitted, the recruiter's queue is the recruiter's queue.

Questions I get a lot

        

          

            Do you charge for a referral?
            

              Never. Referrals are free, and you should not pay anyone for one.
              I do offer paid mentorship, resume reviews and mock interviews on
              Topmate, but those are a completely separate thing and buying one
              does not get you a referral.
            

          

          

            Why email and not LinkedIn?
            

              Because I do not keep up with LinkedIn messages, and I would rather
              tell you that than let your request sit unread in a folder I rarely
              open. Email I actually go through. Feel free to connect with me on
              LinkedIn, just do not send the referral request there.
            

          

          

            I emailed and heard nothing. Should I follow up?
            

              Yes, once, after a couple of weeks. Replying to the same email thread
              is ideal, since it keeps your resume and the job ID together. A
              single polite follow-up genuinely helps; repeated daily pings have
              the opposite effect.
            

          

          

            I am a fresher or from a non-CS background. Is it worth asking?
            

              Absolutely, as long as there is an open role at the right level. What
              I need to see is evidence you can build: projects you actually
              shipped, code I can read, a clear explanation of what you made and
              why. That matters far more to me than your degree.
            

          

          

            Can you review my resume before I apply?
            

              A quick sanity check as part of a referral conversation, usually yes.
              A detailed line-by-line review is what the paid resume review on
              Topmate is for, simply because it takes real time to do properly.
            

          

          

            What if I am not a match for anything right now?
            

              Then I will say so, and I would rather do that than leave you
              waiting. Ask me again in a few months with the gaps closed. Nobody
              gets one shot at this.
